Zap Board

Your references, inside Blender. A board that lives in your .blend — drop images in, arrange and mark them up, pull a palette or send a plane into the scene, without leaving the window you are working in.

01The problem

Your reference lives in another window.

It sits in a browser tab or on a second monitor, so every glance is a context switch — and the file you ship has no memory that the reference was ever there.

Zap Board puts the board on the same canvas as the work and saves it inside the .blend, so it opens with the scene on any machine.

02The board

Drop it. Arrange it. Keep working.

Drag images onto the board and Arrange lays them out for you. Move, scale and align stay under your hands — it behaves like the editor you already know, not a modal dialog you have to fight.

03Compare

Read your work against the reference.

Lay your render over the board, drop its opacity, then switch both to grayscale to check values. The oldest trick in the book, without ever leaving Blender.

04ColourPro

Pull a palette off any reference.

Extract the dominant colours as swatch cards you can keep on the board, then pick a colour straight off the image into whatever you are shading.

05AdjustPro

Brighten a reference that is too dark.

Brightness, contrast, temperature and saturation live on the card and update as you drag — and never touch the file on disk.

06Send to 3DPro

Put a reference into the scene.

Turn a card into an upright image plane, facing front in the viewport — modelling reference you can orbit around and line your mesh up against.

07Also in 2.0

The rest of what 2.0 adds.

Draw on top.

Arrows, boxes and ellipses to call out exactly what matters, in the colours you pick.

A board per shot.

Keep separate boards in one file and switch between them — characters on one, environment on the next.

Paste straight off the web.

Copy an image address, paste it onto the board, and it downloads and lands as a card — the one time anything goes online, and only when you ask.

Group what belongs together.

Named frames hold a cluster of cards and move as one, so a set of references stays a set.

Video and GIF references.Pro

Scrub a clip on the board and pull any frame out as a still to keep beside it.

10Questions

Questions people ask first

Where do the boards live?

Inside your .blend file — images, notes and frames together. Open it on another machine and the board is still there, and you can keep several boards in one file.

What is the difference between Free and Pro?

Free is a complete reference board. Pro adds the colour tools, image adjust, video and GIF, layout templates, send-to-3D and board export — a one-time $5 unlock in this same listing.

Does it work on Mac and Linux?

Yes, all three platforms. The only exception is Reveal File, which opens the folder on Windows only.
