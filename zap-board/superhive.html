Zap Board

A reference board built into Blender. Drop your images in, arrange them, mark them up — and they save inside your .blend. No second window, no folder of loose files, no alt-tab.

01The problem

Your references live in another window.

They sit in a browser, an image viewer, a second monitor — anywhere but the file you are working in. You alt-tab to check a pose, alt-tab back, and when you open the scene on another machine the references are not there.

Zap Board puts the board inside Blender's Image Editor. Drop images onto it, move them like cards on a table, and keep working without leaving the app.

02Arrange & group

Lay it out the way you think.

Drag references anywhere. Select a few and Arrange packs them tidily; Align and Distribute straighten a row. Draw a frame around a set, name it, give it a colour, and move the whole group as one — characters here, environments there.

Add notes, arrows and shapes on top, crop a card down to the part you care about, and set any image's opacity so it reads as a background or an overlay.

03It saves with your file

The board is part of the .blend.

A board belongs to the file, not to your computer. Save the .blend and the board saves with it — every image, note and frame. Open the file next week, or on another machine, and it is all still there.

Keep more than one board per file and switch between them. Nothing to export, no sidecar folder to carry around, and it never touches your scene, your render or your objects.

04Compare your work

Put your WIP over the reference.

Drop a screenshot of your work onto the board, drag it over the reference and lower its opacity to see the two lined up. Flip the board to grayscale and the values jump out with the colour taken away — the quickest way to catch a shape that has drifted.

05Colour toolsPro

Pull a palette off any reference.

Select an image and Extract Palette lays its main colours out as swatch cards next to it. Pick a single colour straight off any card with the eyedropper. Sort a shelf of references by colour to group the warm ones apart from the cool.

06Image adjustPro

Brighten a dark reference in place.

Push brightness, contrast, temperature and saturation on any card right on the board. A photo that is too dark to read becomes usable without opening another editor, and the file on disk is never changed — the adjustment lives on the board.

07Video & GIFPro

Motion reference, on the board.

Add a video or a GIF and it plays right there, with a scrub bar and transport controls. Step frame by frame to study a walk cycle or a piece of acting, and turn any frame into a still card you can keep and mark up.

08Send to the 3D viewPro

Move a reference into the scene.

Select cards on the board and send them to the 3D viewport as reference images, placed at the cursor. Line up a concept next to your model while you block it out, then take them back out with one button when you are done.

11Questions

Questions people ask first

Where do the boards live?

Inside your .blend file. Save the file and the board is saved with it — images, notes and frames. Open it anywhere and it is all still there. You can keep several boards in one file.

Does it change my scene or my files?

No. The board is its own thing; it does not touch your objects, your render or the image files on disk. Image adjustments live on the board, not on the original file.

What is the difference between Free and Pro?

Free is a complete reference board — arrange, group, mark up, crop, opacity, grayscale, many boards. Pro adds the colour tools, video and GIF playback, image adjust, layout templates, and sending references to the 3D view. Pro is a one-time $5 unlock.

Does it work on Mac and Linux?

Yes. The whole add-on runs on Windows, macOS and Linux. The only exception is Reveal File, which opens a folder in the OS file browser and is Windows only.

How do I open a board?

In Blender's Image Editor, switch on Zap Board. Shift+F10 flips the editor over to it. Everything is in the side panel from there.
