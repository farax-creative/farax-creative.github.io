Prompt Motion

Your character has to walk in, hesitate, and sit down. That clip isn't in the library, and hand-keying it is an afternoon. Type the sentence instead. Prompt Motion generates the motion and keys it onto the armature you already have.

01The problem

Getting motion onto your character is two problems, not one.

Finding a clip is the first. The second is that whatever you find was built for someone else's skeleton, so the afternoon goes to bone mapping, a twist chain that flips, and feet that slide.

Prompt Motion does both in one step. You type what happens; it generates the motion and bakes it onto the armature you have selected, as an ordinary Action you can edit in the Dope Sheet.

02Key poses

Set two poses. It animates the in-between.

Pose the character where the shot starts. Move a few seconds on and pose it where the shot ends. Type what happens in between, and Generate builds that motion so it passes through both of your poses.

The frames you care about stay yours. Add Key Here works on any frame, so a third or fourth pose is one more click.

03Workflow

Three steps, and the first one happens once.

  1. 01Install the runtime once. One button, then 10–30 minutes while it sets up Python and fetches the model. No account, no sign-in, no key to paste.
  2. 02Select your armature and type. “walks forward and sits down on a chair.” Prompts in Korean and Chinese are translated before generation.
  3. 03Press Generate. The clip is retargeted and keyed onto your rig as an Action. Retime it, layer it, push it to the NLA, throw half of it away.
04Retargeting

Your own character or a Mixamo download. It just works.

No renaming and no bone lists: the mapping is worked out for you. Matching runs on bone names and skeleton structure, so rigs that never followed a convention still map. Presets ship for Mixamo, VRoid, MMD, Rigify, CloudRig, Auto-Rig Pro and DAZ Genesis 8 and 9, and every row is editable and saveable as your own preset. Presets live outside the add-on folder, so an update never eats them.

05Hands

Fingers are keyed with the body.

A generated clip normally arrives with flat hands, because motion models key the body and leave the fingers at rest. Prompt Motion poses them from the rig's own finger bones, left and right independently: three poses, no rigging required.

The thumb is not given an angle. It is solved onto the fingers your rig actually has, so its pad comes to rest on them instead of hanging in the air — measured at one finger radius on every rig tested.

06Safety

It refuses rather than guesses.

If the mapping can't cover the joints the engine needs, the panel names the bones it could not match and stops, instead of baking a broken take onto your character.

A Rigify or CloudRig limb left on IK ignores the FK keys this writes, and the chain stretches. So the panel tells you before you generate rather than after, and one button switches the limbs. It never flips your rig's own logic behind your back.

07Library, paths, goals

Ten motions work the minute it's installed.

Walk, run, idle, wave, jump, look around, clap, bow, pick up and phone call come with the add-on. Pick one in the Library and press Apply. It lands on your rig with no server, no GPU and no download. Everything you generate later goes into the same Library.

Draw a curve and the character walks it. Pick a pace (walk, brisk walk or run) and the clip length follows the path. Ask a walk to cover ground at running speed and the panel tells you before you generate.

Or give it a Goal. Plan Route walks the character there, around the objects you mark to avoid.

08Offline

Your prompts stay on your machine.

The model runs locally, against a server you start yourself on localhost. There is no account, no queue, and no cost per generation. The tenth clip costs what the first one did.

One exception, stated plainly: a prompt in another language is translated by a local model that downloads once (3 or 15 GB). Until it has, a built-in dictionary covers common motions. Nothing goes online unless you choose the free online translator in Preferences yourself.

10Questions

Questions people ask first

Is this a web service with a Blender button?

No. Generation runs against a server you start yourself on localhost. English prompts never leave your machine. The one translation exception is described above.

Will it work on my rig?

Matching by name and by structure, eight presets, and every row editable by hand. If it cannot map the core joints it tells you which ones and refuses to bake.

What if I don't like the result?

It's an Action. Delete it, or keep the parts you want. Generating again does not overwrite the previous clip.

My character has no rig yet.

Then rig it first. Prompt Motion animates an armature; it does not build one, and with a mesh selected and no armature the buttons stay greyed out rather than guessing. You do not have to go through Mixamo: Rigify ships free with Blender and has a preset here, CloudRig is free from Blender Studio and has one too, Mixamo's web auto-rigger is free and its output has a preset, and Auto-Rig Pro is a paid add-on whose rigs the matcher reads.

Do I need a HuggingFace account?

No. The model downloads from a public mirror, and the installer never asks for a token.
